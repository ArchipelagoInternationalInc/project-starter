# 05 — THE PORTFOLIO RULES
*Archipelago Studio Kit v2 · How the whole studio decides what to work on. These rules exist because attention, not ideas, is the scarce resource here.*

---

## The goals, in order

1. **Ship finished products that make money.** Finished and selling beats advanced and parked.
2. **Client work runs reliably** — the content factory at capacity, client sites delivered to their written scope.
3. Everything else is inventory.

## The ceiling

**Three active workstreams plus client work. Never more.** Within that, **one "pre-revenue build slot"**: only one not-yet-earning product gets serious building attention at a time. When it ships (or fails its gate), the next project in line takes the slot — a deliberate handoff, ruled in the PM chat, never a drift.

A shelf full of plans is an asset, not a to-do list: **build packets are inventory, not obligation.** Nothing on the shelf nags.

## Client rules

- **The written one-page scope IS the engagement.** Deliverables, cadence, price, and boundaries on one page before work starts — even for tiny clients, even for friends, even for trades. "And more" is never a scope. Requests beyond the page go to Scott, not quietly into the work.
- **Client information is isolated absolutely.** Private repositories, private reports, no client names in anything public.
- Per-client hard rules (the promises that define the account) are written in bold in that client's files and never bent. Example on record: for the cat sanctuary client, real cats only, identified by name — never AI-generated cats presented as adoptable.

## How truth is organized (three layers)

- **GitHub** holds the work itself and the session reports — what was actually built and done.
- **Google Drive** holds the studio's documents — plans, scopes, letters, and the one-page status document.
- **Flightdeck** (the studio's own dashboard) shows humans and clients where things stand — once its data-lock work is complete.

When layers disagree, the work itself wins: **documents describe intentions; the project folder describes reality.** When in doubt, look at the folder.

## The cadence

- Weekly: Scott says **"weekly review"** → the PM reads everything fresh, issues per-project directions (next task, blockers, what to stop doing), and rewrites the dated status document.
- Anytime: **"check"** on any project → the PM reads that project's new reports and produces the next instruction.
- The status document is perishable on purpose. It is replaced whole, never patched, and old copies are trashed so exactly one truth exists.

## Deadline discipline

Dates are commitments to ourselves. A date that can't be met is **re-cut on purpose, in the PM chat, with a reason** — a re-cut date is a plan; a slid date is a debt. Personal immovables (taxes, the cruise) sit on the same board as project dates and are defended the same way.

## Verification before speech

The PM states nothing as fact that it hasn't read or measured itself — it inspects the folder, the report, or the dashboard first, then speaks. Twice this studio paid for confident claims made from memory; the correction is permanent: **verify with tools, then speak.** The same standard binds every seat, including Scott's AIs when talking to each other.

## Changing these rules

Any rule here can be changed — by Scott, on purpose, recorded in an update to this kit. No rule changes by exception, workaround, or forgetting.
