# 03 — WHAT WE SAY IN PUBLIC
*Archipelago Studio Kit v2 · Every public sentence, every product, every client. Honesty is our operating system, not our slogan — it has repeatedly been cheaper than the repair it prevented.*

---

## The owner's veto

**Scott approves every public sentence and every dollar.** No page, post, price, headline, or email goes public without his approval. Approved wording lives in a written, per-project bank of approved copy; anything not in the bank is not published.

## The quarantine rule

Unapproved words don't merely *wait* — they are kept somewhere they **physically cannot ship** from. On any project with a website build, an automatic check blocks publishing if forbidden wording or unapproved copy appears. Rules this important get mechanisms, not reminders.

## What we never say

- **Never a legal conclusion as a promise.** We never say a product makes anyone "compliant," and never guarantee outcomes ("guaranteed complete," "catches everything," "100%"). We say what the tool does, what it was independently measured to do, and what it cannot do.
- **Never the appearance of government affiliation.** No official-looking seals, names, or wording.
- **Never medical, legal, or financial advice claims,** on any product, ever.
- **Never invented proof.** No fake testimonials, no made-up statistics, no "customers love us" before customers exist.

## Numbers and facts

- **Every public number traces to a real recorded measurement,** and gets re-derived when the measurement changes. If the measurement doesn't exist, the number doesn't get published.
- **Dated legal facts (deadlines, regulations) get a guard** — a check that blocks publishing if a superseded date reappears. We learned this the hard way: the law changed while our copy stood still, and our site served outdated deadline dates until a routine check caught it.
- **Citations are verified against the actual source documents,** by script where possible, never from memory. A near-identical wrong citation was once one careless paste from shipping.
- **Outside reviews are cheap and catch real errors.** Before launch, buy fresh eyes (other AI reviewers, a second Claude) — then judge their findings against our own rulebook rather than swallowing them whole.

## Honesty as strategy

- **Say plainly what the product cannot do.** Published limits are a trust engine, not a weakness.
- **Decline honestly, never charge for a decline.** Where a product can refuse a job it can't do well, the refusal must be free — and where possible, built so charging a declined customer is *impossible by construction*, not by policy.
- **Trust Scott's read of the customer.** His marketing instincts have repeatedly beaten the standard playbook (dropping the email wall, refusing collect-and-blast email, betting on search over ads). When his instinct sets a direction, the studio's job is to translate it into machinery.

## Saying it straight, between ourselves too

Every seat — chats and Builders alike — **labels inference as inference.** "I believe" and "unverified" are required words when the source is a guess, however plausible. One confident invented claim once cratered the owner's trust at the worst possible moment; the repair took a full retraction plus an item-by-item inventory of what was actually verified, with sources. That's the standing repair, too: when caught wrong, restate what *is* known, item by item, with receipts. Trust is rebuilt with receipts, not apologies.

## Client work

The same rules apply to anything published for a client, plus: **the client's world is confidential by default.** Client names, materials, and information never appear in public folders, reports, or examples without the client's written OK. And the studio's own scope discipline is a public-honesty rule too: what we promised is what the one-page scope says — nothing silently more, nothing silently less.
