Create one continuous, full-length homepage design—not merely a hero section.

The final result should look like a complete website being viewed from the top of the page to the footer. It must be organized as a sequence of substantial, full-width horizontal bands that scroll vertically and tell a persuasive sales story.

STRUCTURAL PRINCIPLE

Think of the website as a sequence of individual advertising scenes.

Every section should:

1. Communicate one important idea.
2. Have its own headline, short supporting copy, and visual focal point.
3. Feel visually distinct from the section before and after it.
4. Still belong to the same unified design system.
5. Lead naturally into the next section.
6. Help move the visitor closer to taking the primary action.

Do not create a single oversized hero followed by a grid of small generic cards.

Do not create an infographic poster.

Do not create a dashboard collage with every feature shown at once.

Do not compress the entire product story into one screen.

The page must feel intentionally paced, like a premium editorial presentation, an advertising campaign, or a highly art-directed corporate website.

FORMAT AND CANVAS

Create a high-resolution, full-page desktop website composition.

Use an approximately 1440–1600 pixel desktop width.

The full composition should be approximately 8–12 browser screens tall.

Show the page continuously from:

Header
to hero
to feature bands
to proof and trust
to final call to action
to footer.

The homepage should be long enough that every major product benefit receives its own properly designed section.

GLOBAL DESIGN DIRECTION

The visual quality should feel:

• Award-winning
• Contemporary
• Expensive
• Strategic
• Highly art-directed
• Conversion-focused
• Suitable for implementation in Next.js
• Enhanced by tasteful GSAP animation
• Polished enough to present to investors, customers, or a major national client

Use a disciplined design system rather than a collection of unrelated ideas.

Create:

• A consistent typography hierarchy
• A consistent button system
• A consistent icon style
• A consistent UI mockup style
• A consistent lighting and rendering style
• Repeating spacing and alignment rules
• Clear visual rhythm
• Strong contrast between major sections
• Large, confident headlines
• Generous margins and breathing room
• Deliberate transitions between sections

Avoid generic startup-template aesthetics.

Avoid excessive pill-shaped labels, random floating cards, gratuitous gradients, meaningless statistics, and decorative interface elements that do not help explain the product.

PAGE NARRATIVE

The page should follow this persuasive sequence:

1. PROMISE
2. PROBLEM
3. SOLUTION
4. PRODUCT EXPERIENCE
5. FEATURE BENEFITS
6. HOW IT WORKS
7. SECURITY OR TRUST
8. CUSTOMER PROOF
9. FINAL CONVERSION
10. FOOTER

Each of these should appear as one or more full-width website bands.

SECTION 1 — HEADER AND HERO

Create a premium navigation header with:

• Product logo at left
• Concise navigation in the center or right
• Sign-in link if relevant
• One visually dominant primary CTA
• Excellent alignment and restrained spacing

The hero should immediately explain:

• What the product is
• Who it is for
• What outcome it delivers
• What the visitor should do next

Use a large, memorable headline with a clear hierarchy.

The hero must contain:

• One concise audience or category label
• One dominant headline
• One short supporting paragraph
• Primary CTA
• Secondary CTA
• One principal product visual

The principal visual may be:

• A smartphone held by a person
• A laptop or browser product mockup
• A product photograph
• A sophisticated conceptual visualization
• A combination of desktop and mobile UI

The hero visual must be clear, polished, and believable.

Do not let the visual overpower the message.

Do not place tiny unreadable text throughout the product mockup.

SECTION 2 — THE CUSTOMER PROBLEM

Create a full-width band dramatizing the customer’s current frustration.

This section should make the visitor think:

“That is exactly what happens to me.”

Use a visually different composition from the hero.

Possible structures:

• Product clutter on one side and explanatory copy on the other
• A fragmented workflow illustrated across several objects
• A before-state shown through files, messages, spreadsheets, deadlines, or physical paperwork
• A cinematic photographic scene with restrained overlays

The section should contain:

• Small category label
• Strong problem-focused headline
• Short explanatory paragraph
• Three or four specific pain points

Keep the problem concrete rather than abstract.

Do not overuse fear.

The purpose is recognition, not panic.

SECTION 3 — THE CORE SOLUTION

Introduce the product as the organizing system that replaces the problem.

Use a substantial, elegant product dashboard or central interface as the main visual.

This section should feel calmer, more ordered, and more controlled than the problem section.

Present the central product promise in a simple form such as:

• Everything in one place
• One view of the entire workflow
• One organized system
• One secure source of truth

Include three to five concise outcome statements around or beside the interface.

The product UI must use the same design language as the hero UI.

Do not invent a second unrelated interface style.

SECTION 4 — FEATURE BAND ONE

Dedicate an entire horizontal band to the first major product capability.

Suggested structure:

• Copy on the left
• Large product visualization on the right

Or reverse the arrangement for visual rhythm.

Show the feature in use rather than presenting a generic icon.

Possible feature examples:

• Uploading and organizing materials
• Managing records
• Searching a library
• Viewing a dashboard
• Monitoring a project
• Reviewing upcoming deadlines

Include:

• Eyebrow label
• Feature headline
• One short paragraph
• Three clear benefit points
• One large product interaction or UI state

SECTION 5 — FEATURE BAND TWO

Create another full-width section for the second major product capability.

Reverse the layout from the previous section.

This creates an alternating editorial rhythm as the visitor scrolls.

Give this section its own visual concept while preserving the product UI system.

Examples:

• Calendar and reminders
• Status monitoring
• Automated notifications
• Team collaboration
• Progress tracking
• Analytics
• Personalization

Show a focused interface state rather than an entire dashboard repeated again.

SECTION 6 — FEATURE BAND THREE / WORKFLOW

Use this band to demonstrate the product’s defining action or “money feature.”

This should be one of the strongest sections on the page.

Show the process sequentially:

Step 1
Step 2
Step 3
Result

Use connected interface panels, an animated pathway, a horizontal process, or a central action surrounded by supporting steps.

The process must be understandable without reading long paragraphs.

The visitor should understand:

• What they select
• What the system creates or does
• What happens next
• What result they receive

Make the process feel simple, fast, and professional.

SECTION 7 — RESULT OR DELIVERY

Create a section focused on the final customer outcome.

Do not merely repeat the workflow.

Show what success looks like after the product has completed its job.

Possible outcomes:

• A finished package
• A delivered message
• A completed transaction
• An organized project
• A hiring manager receiving information
• A customer receiving confirmation
• A team viewing a completed result
• A clean before-and-after comparison

This section should sell the emotional payoff:

• Less scrambling
• More confidence
• Faster action
• Better presentation
• Greater control
• More professional results

SECTION 8 — SECURITY, TRUST, OR CONTROL

Create a darker, quieter, highly credible trust section.

Explain why the visitor can safely rely on the product.

Use concrete visual demonstrations such as:

• Private storage
• Access permissions
• Authentication
• Expiring links
• Activity history
• Export controls
• Deletion controls
• Data ownership
• Delivery records

Do not rely only on generic padlock icons.

Show actual product controls or an understandable systems diagram.

Avoid unsupported certifications, invented legal claims, and decorative seals.

Never imply that the product verifies, certifies, approves, or guarantees something unless that is factually true.

SECTION 9 — CUSTOMER PROOF

Create a premium proof band containing only credible claims supplied in the project brief.

Possible elements:

• One strong customer quotation
• Customer portrait or contextual image
• Selected client or partner names
• Short use-case story
• Measurable result
• Product adoption statistic
• Review score, only when verified
• Before-and-after result

Do not invent:

• Customer counts
• Star ratings
• Hospitals
• Corporate logos
• Certifications
• Awards
• Usage totals
• Testimonials
• Performance percentages

When verified proof is not supplied, use clearly marked placeholder content or design the section as “What early users value” without numerical claims.

SECTION 10 — FINAL CONVERSION BAND

Create a visually strong closing section that summarizes the promise and presents one clear next action.

This section should feel conclusive and optimistic.

Include:

• Large closing headline
• One short supporting paragraph
• Primary CTA
• Optional secondary CTA
• One low-friction reassurance, such as “No card required,” only when accurate
• A compact final product or customer visual

The final CTA should visually echo the hero CTA so the page feels intentionally bookended.

SECTION 11 — FOOTER

Create a complete professional footer containing:

• Logo
• One-sentence product description
• Product links
• Company links
• Resources
• Legal links
• Contact information
• Social links where appropriate
• Copyright
• Required disclaimer
• Optional email signup

The footer must feel designed, not appended as an afterthought.

VISUAL RHYTHM

Alternate the section compositions as the visitor scrolls.

For example:

Hero:
Text left, product right

Problem:
Visual left, text right

Core solution:
Text left, wide interface right

Feature one:
Large UI left, copy right

Feature two:
Copy left, visual right

Workflow:
Full-width sequential composition

Security:
Copy left, connected control panels right

Proof:
Testimonial right, customer image or product context left

Final CTA:
Centered or split composition

Do not repeat the exact same two-column layout in every section.

Use controlled variation:

• Some sections may be centered.
• Some may be asymmetrical.
• Some may use a full-bleed visual.
• Some may use a contained framed panel.
• Some may use a wide interface spanning most of the page.
• Some may use a smaller product detail enlarged dramatically.

BAND TRANSITIONS

Make every major section clearly identifiable as its own band.

Use combinations of:

• Background-value changes
• Subtle border lines
• Soft glows
• Controlled gradients
• Curved or diagonal transitions
• Large abstract forms
• Slight shifts in content width
• Framed editorial panels
• Full-bleed imagery
• Spacious negative space

Avoid abrupt, accidental transitions.

The page should feel like one continuous composition with deliberate chapters.

NEXT.JS AND GSAP MOTION DIRECTION

Design the static page so it clearly supports sophisticated motion when implemented.

Suggested motion behavior:

• Hero text reveals in controlled staggered lines
• Main device enters with slight depth and parallax
• Background orbit lines move slowly
• Product cards subtly separate as the user scrolls
• Individual feature bands use pinned or sticky product visuals
• Interface screenshots crossfade or slide between states
• Workflow connectors animate from one step to the next
• Numbers count upward only when factual
• Security pathways illuminate as they enter the viewport
• Final CTA resolves into a calm, stable composition

Motion must support comprehension.

Do not use constant floating, bouncing, spinning, or excessive mouse-following effects.

The page should remain elegant with animation disabled.

PRODUCT UI RULES

Create a coherent application UI rather than unrelated mockups.

Use:

• The same logo
• The same navigation language
• The same typography
• The same radius system
• The same icon family
• The same button system
• The same status treatment
• The same mobile and desktop components
• Consistent names, dates, labels, and example data

Every UI screen should represent a different state of the same real product.

Interface text should be concise and legible.

Do not create fictional functionality that is not included in the supplied product brief.

COPY RULES

Use the supplied website copy whenever it is available.

Do not rewrite factual product claims merely to make them sound more impressive.

Headlines may be sharpened, but the meaning must remain accurate.

Keep paragraphs short.

Write for scanning.

Each section should have:

• One main idea
• One primary headline
• One supporting paragraph
• No more than three or four supporting points

Avoid jargon, empty hype, and broad unsupported superlatives.

Do not use language such as:

• Guaranteed
• Official
• Certified
• Fully compliant
• Never miss
• Always accurate
• Verified

unless the project documentation explicitly proves the claim.

QUALITY-CONTROL CHECKLIST

Before presenting the final design, verify all of the following:

1. It is a complete homepage, not a hero concept.
2. It contains at least eight substantial horizontal bands.
3. Every band communicates one clear product or sales idea.
4. The sections form a logical customer journey.
5. Layouts alternate enough to create visual rhythm.
6. Product UI is consistent across the entire page.
7. The design contains a clear primary CTA near the top and bottom.
8. No unsupported statistics, customers, certifications, or testimonials appear.
9. Text is large enough to be legible in the full-page composition.
10. The footer is included.
11. The design is feasible to implement in Next.js.
12. Motion opportunities are suitable for GSAP but are not required for the static design to work.
13. The overall result feels premium, strategic, modern, and conversion-focused.
14. The design looks like a real website, not a presentation board or infographic.
15. The page uses sequential full-width bands similar to a professionally art-directed corporate homepage.

FINAL OUTPUT

Produce one cohesive, full-length desktop homepage design.

Show the entire page from header through footer in one continuous vertical composition.

Prioritize structure, pacing, hierarchy, and sales storytelling over decorative effects.

The final design should make the viewer understand:

• The customer’s problem
• The product’s solution
• The product’s most important capabilities
• How the product works
• Why it can be trusted
• What result the customer receives
• What action the customer should take next
