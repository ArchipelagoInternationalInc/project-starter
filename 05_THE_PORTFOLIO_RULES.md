# 05 — THE PORTFOLIO RULES
*Archipelago Studio Kit v3 · How the whole studio decides what to work on. These rules exist because attention, not ideas, is the scarce resource here.*

---

## The goals, in order

1. **Ship finished products that make money.** Finished and selling beats advanced and parked.
2. **Client work runs reliably** — the content factory at capacity, client sites delivered to their written scope.
3. Everything else is inventory.

## The lineup (replaces the three-workstream ceiling, October 2026)

Work is organized in tiers, kept in `studio-hq/LINEUP.md` and rewritten only by Scott's ruling:

- **Frontline products** — the few products being finished and released to earn. They get full Builder attention, in the cloud, around the clock.
- **Frontline clients** — paying engagements, run to their written scope. PURRR is first among them.
- **Personal frontline** — the owner's immovables (taxes, the archive). On the same board, defended the same way.
- **Second line** — kept moving with light touches: a status file, a market brief, an outreach inquiry. Promoted when a frontline slot opens.
- **Third line and parked** — one `STATUS.md` each, nothing more. Picked up cold when their turn comes.

The practical ceiling is no longer a number of projects; it is the studio's weekly Claude usage budget and Scott's four hours a day. The Studio Head watches both and says when the frontline is full. **Build packets are inventory, not obligation.** Nothing on the shelf nags.

**Hand-work versus loop-work.** Some frontline work needs Scott's eye (copy, image choice, taste) and runs in the daytime with him. Some can run unattended all night (anything a script can judge). The lineup marks which is which, so the overnight Builders are never waiting on a human and the human is never asked to approve what a script could.

## Client rules

- **The written one-page scope IS the engagement.** Deliverables, cadence, price, and boundaries on one page before work starts — even for tiny clients, even for friends, even for trades. "And more" is never a scope. Requests beyond the page go to Scott, not quietly into the work.
- **Client information is isolated absolutely.** Private repositories, private reports, no client names in anything public.
- Per-client hard rules (the promises that define the account) are written in bold in that client's files and never bent. Example on record: for the cat sanctuary client, real cats only, identified by name — never AI-generated cats presented as adoptable.
- **Madelyn is the account executive for every social-media client.** Client intake, creative, client approval, and publishing run through her account and her phone, with no step waiting on Scott. Scott owns the technical build and the money.

## How truth is organized (three layers)

- **GitHub** holds the work itself, the session reports, the inbound logs, and the decision records — anything a Builder or the Studio Head needs. `studio-hq` holds the lineup and the status board.
- **Google Drive** holds the studio's documents — plans, scopes, letters — and the **media library** for every social-media client.
- **Blotato** holds the record of what was posted and how it performed, alongside the per-client Content Log in Drive.
- **Published dashboards** (Claude's Dashboard pages) show clients where their work stands. Flightdeck remains the studio's own internal dashboard once its data-lock work is complete.

When layers disagree, the work itself wins: **documents describe intentions; the project folder describes reality.** When in doubt, look at the folder.

## The cadence

- Continuously: the Studio Head reads new reports and inbound entries as they land, hands Builders their next Definition of Done, and keeps `STUDIO_STATUS.md` current.
- Weekly: Scott says **"weekly review"** → the Studio Head reads everything fresh, issues per-project directions (next task, blockers, what to stop doing), and rewrites the status board.
- Anytime: Scott reads the board or answers a decision list. He never has to ask what's happening.
- The status board is perishable on purpose. It is replaced whole, never patched, so exactly one truth exists.

## Deadline discipline

Dates are commitments to ourselves. A date that can't be met is **re-cut on purpose, by the Studio Head with Scott's ruling, with a reason** — a re-cut date is a plan; a slid date is a debt. Personal immovables (taxes, the cruise) sit on the same board as project dates and are defended the same way.

## Verification before speech

The Studio Head states nothing as fact that it hasn't read or measured itself — it inspects the folder, the report, or the dashboard first, then speaks. Twice this studio paid for confident claims made from memory; the correction is permanent: **verify with tools, then speak.** The same standard binds every seat, including Scott's AIs when talking to each other.

## Changing these rules

Any rule here can be changed — by Scott, on purpose, recorded in an update to this kit. No rule changes by exception, workaround, or forgetting.
